# Long-form presentation

[Français](PRESENTATION_LONGUE.md) | **English**

## Constitutive theory of relational determination

This theory starts from a principle: **before representing, measuring, evaluating, or aligning an object, one must establish what constitutes it and what makes it possible to identify it through its transformations.**

Its central objects are **relational constitutive roles**: structural determinations established for individuated occurrences from the relations they maintain within a construction.

The general order of the theory is:

```text
constitution
→ relational constitutive roles
→ dependencies
→ method of determination
→ reconstruction
→ constitutive boundary
→ identity
→ persistence
→ alignment
```

Circularity plays a central role in this framework, not as the general foundation of the theory, but as an **instance in which the whole architecture can be deployed and tested**.

### Scientific positioning

This theory lies close to work on organizational closure, relational wholes, individuation, structural boundaries, and identity. Its specific contribution does not lie in any one of these notions taken in isolation, but in the constructive order that connects them: reconstruction of a constitutive domain, determination of what is new relative to that domain, formation of a regime boundary, genesis of a fresh identity, and coherent persistence of that identity. The detailed comparative positioning is presented in [SCIENTIFIC_POSITIONING.md](SCIENTIFIC_POSITIONING.md).

## 1. Constitution

The first problem is:

> What makes an object this object within a given construction?

The answer is relational.

An occurrence is not first determined by a value, a representation, or a label. It is individuated by its formation and by the constitutive relations it maintains in the construction: provenance, source and target relations, position, succession, composition, and participation.

Constitution therefore comes before readout, representation, and status.

```text
constitutive relations
        ↓
individuation of the occurrence
        ↓
constitutive determination
```

This priority then makes the question of identity possible: one must first establish **what is determined** before asking whether that determination persists.

---

## 2. Relational constitutive roles

A **relational constitutive role** is the structural determination established for an individuated occurrence from the relations it maintains and the place it occupies within a construction.

The term **constitutive** indicates that these relations participate in what makes it possible to determine the occurrence within the construction.

The term **relational** indicates that this determination is reducible neither to an intrinsic property, nor to a label, nor to a readout added afterward.

The theory aims to establish:

```text
individuated occurrences
        ↓
relational constitutive roles
        ↓
conditions of their identity
        ↓
conditions of persistence
```

Determining a relational constitutive role also requires distinguishing the relations necessary for that determination from candidate properties whose status must be established: whether they must be posited as primitive or can be derived from a more fundamental structure.

A candidate property must therefore not be added merely because it is useful in a proof. One must determine what it depends on.

```text
candidate property
        ↓
dependency analysis
        ↓
primitive
or
reconstructible
```

These dependencies impose an **ordering of the construction**.

If a determination `B` depends on `A`, then `A` must be established before `B`.

```text
A
↓
B
```

What depends on a determination cannot be used retroactively to constitute that on which it depends.

The theory therefore does not seek to accumulate properties, but to establish **the actual order of constitutive dependencies**.

---

## 3. Method for determining dependencies

Relational constitutive roles are theoretical objects. The method serves to determine which structures are actually necessary to establish them.

The methodological principle is:

```text
weaken
→ separate
→ reconstruct
```

One starts from a structure in which a result is provable.

One then removes some data suspected not to be primitive.

If the result remains derivable, those data were not necessary at that level.

If the result ceases to be derivable, one seeks a **separating model** that preserves the properties already established while making the candidate property fail.

The method then consists in identifying the dependencies whose necessity can be established by separation within the class of structures under consideration.

```text
rich structure
        ↓ weakening
weaker structure
        ↓
result still derivable?
     /              \
   yes              no
    ↓                ↓
dependency       explicit
unnecessary      separator
at this level        ↓
                 dependency established
                 by separation
```

Separators thus establish precise non-implications and localize the dependencies actually used by the formalized constructions. They do not claim to establish absolute minimality among all possible formalizations or all possible proofs.

Finally, when additional conditions are reintroduced, one seeks to show that the richer structure can be **reconstructed** from them.

The distinction between primitive and reconstructible is therefore not verbal. It is established by positive constructions, separators, and reconstruction theorems.

This method avoids two opposite errors: assuming too much structure from the outset, or claiming to derive a property that the available dependencies do not suffice to determine.

## 4. Circularity as the central instance

Circularity is the principal instance in which this method can be followed from local exactness to a global constitutive boundary.

The general chain is:

```text
minimal local exactness
        ↓
derived injectivity
        ↓
derived order and adjacency
        ↓
perimeter reconstruction
        ↓
positive continuation
        ↓
old / new separation
        ↓
residual determination
        ↓
uniqueness of the residual occurrence
        ↓
interpretation of the positive boundary
        ↓
rejection of totalization
        ↓
exact regime classification
        ↓
relative maximality of the perimeter
```

### 4.1 Minimal local constitutive exactness

The starting point is not a complete global description of the perimeter.

A local realization associates occurrences with non-closing constitutive requirements and requires only exact structural agreement on their source address.

This minimal agreement is then sufficient to reconstruct more structure.

```text
exact local constitutive agreement
        ↓
exact source state
        ↓
exact target state
        ↓
exact local step
```

Injectivity therefore does not need to be postulated separately in this situation.

It becomes a consequence of structural exactness and of the impossibility for two distinct depths to share the same constitutive path.

### 4.2 Derived order and adjacency

In a genuinely generated history, local constitutive exactness is also sufficient to reconstruct the order among occurrences.

The structural precedence of the perimeter requirements is preserved.

Canonical adjacency is preserved as well.

```text
local constitutive exactness
        ↓
injectivity
        ↓
precedence
        ↓
adjacency
```

These dependencies are not tautological.

When the carrier is weakened by abandoning the global history structure, one can preserve an exact and injective local realization while permuting the order of occurrences.

One can also preserve order while interleaving additional occurrences that do not participate in the required constitutive bridge.

These separators show that:

```text
local exactness
≠ automatic order on every carrier

positional order
≠ constitutive participation
```

Reconstruction therefore depends on the structure actually available.

### 4.3 Perimeter reconstruction

When exact occurrences live in a genuine generated history, the local structure can be reconstructed globally.

The perimeter occurrences make it possible to factor the history recursively.

One then obtains the **canonical perimeter reconstructed as an initial factor** of the history under consideration.

```text
exact local occurrences
        ↓
structural factorization
        ↓
canonical perimeter
reconstructed as a prefix
of the history
```

This is not a reconstruction by counting.

Numerical depth or length is not needed at this stage.

The reconstruction is first constitutive and relational.

The expression **exact reconstruction of the perimeter** refers here to the exactness of this structural factorization. It does not mean that the perimeter is defined by an exact numerical length.

### 4.3.1 Constitutive function of the reconstructed perimeter

The reconstructed perimeter is not sought as a size measure or as a totality of everything that can be constructed.

It provides a **canonical structural boundary inside the history**.

This boundary performs several successive functions.

First, it delimits the part of the construction in which the internal roles have already been realized.

It then makes it possible to distinguish occurrences belonging to that constituted part from occurrences introduced by a continuation.

```text
history
=
reconstructed perimeter
+
continuation
```

Therefore:

```text
perimeter occurrences
        =
occurrences already constituted relative
to the internal roles

continuation occurrences
        =
occurrences new relative
to that constitution
```

This distinction is indispensable to the notion of residuality.

An occurrence can be called residual only relative to a structure in which the internal roles have already been determined and realized.

The perimeter provides precisely that reference structure.

It then performs a second function: it provides the **canonical representative relative to which the regime can be classified**.

Finally, in the theory of persistence, the occurrences of this perimeter provide the initial carrier of the circular instance from which already constituted identities can be followed under extension.

Thus:

```text
reconstructed canonical perimeter
        │
        ├─ delimits already realized internal roles
        │
        ├─ separates old and new
        │       ↓
        │   makes residual determination possible
        │
        ├─ provides the canonical regime representative
        │       ↓
        │   makes classification possible
        │
        └─ provides the initial occurrences
                ↓
            support for persistence
```

The perimeter therefore plays a hinge role: **it transforms a collection of local agreements into a structural boundary from which the new, the residual, regime maximality, and identity persistence can be defined.**

**The reconstructed perimeter thus constitutes a constitutive whole: a structural unity whose constitutive relations suffice to determine canonically its own domain of interiority. Its completeness consists in this positive determination of the internal domain.**

Circularity thus supplies a relational closure sufficient for the internal domain to be delimited by a canonical perimeter. This closure is not, however, a totalization of everything that can be constructed. The perimeter is complete relative to the already constituted internal roles even though generation can continue beyond it. It is this gap between constitutive completeness and possible continuation that makes the residual possible and, subsequently, makes a regime boundary determinable.

### 4.4 The perimeter does not stop generation

Once the canonical perimeter has been reconstructed, the free construction can still continue.

There is a strict continuation beyond the perimeter.

```text
canonical perimeter
        ↓
generation
        ↓
strict continuation
```

One must therefore not speak of a halt of the construction.

The relevant distinction is between:

```text
possibility of continuing the construction
≠
possibility of remaining in the same regime
```

This difference is what makes it possible to define a genuine constitutive boundary.

### 4.5 Residual determination

**Relative to the reconstructed perimeter**, the continuation introduces a new part distinct from the occurrences already present.

The distinction between old and new is therefore not added from the outside: it results from the structural factorization of the history into perimeter and continuation.

When this old / new separation is equipped with faithful role labelling, a new occurrence cannot reuse an internal constitutive role already assigned to the perimeter.

```text
old
≠
new

+
faithful labelling
        ↓
impossibility for the new occurrence
to reuse an internal role
        ↓
residual role
```

Once reuse of internal roles has been excluded for new occurrences, contractibility of the residual-role type forces every residual role obtained to coincide with the same distinguished role.

The residual role is therefore not an arbitrary label added after the continuation. It is obtained by excluding the internal roles and determining the only residual role available.

### 4.6 Uniqueness of the residual occurrence

The residual-determination core first establishes that every new occurrence carries the same distinguished residual role.

When the labelling of new occurrences is injective, two new occurrences carrying that same role cannot remain distinct.

```text
exclusion of internal roles
+
contractibility of the residual role
        ↓
the same residual role
for every new occurrence
        ↓
faithfulness of the labelling
        ↓
one residual occurrence
```

A positive continuation therefore has a distinguished residual occurrence and no other distinct new occurrence.

In the canonical one-step instance, this occurrence is precisely the unique occurrence added to the perimeter.

### 4.7 From the residual to regime classification

The reconstructed perimeter now provides the **canonical reference relative to which admission can be classified**.

The residual participates directly in the mechanism that makes this classification possible.

A positive extension first supplies its residual-determination core.

This core produces a unique residual occurrence.

That occurrence is then used to construct the interpretation of the positive boundary.

From this interpretation, a totalization attempt is formulated.

The totalization attempt then seeks to absorb this residual occurrence into a complete closure of the regime. In the circular instance, such an absorption would contract the constitutive difference preserved by the construction.

The already preserved constitutive obstruction makes it possible to reject that attempt.

```text
positive continuation
        ↓
residual core
        ↓
unique residual occurrence
        ↓
boundary interpretation
        ↓
totalization attempt
        ↓
rejection by the constitutive obstruction
```

This rejection eliminates the positive branch of an admitted candidate.

It then makes it possible to obtain the **exact classification of the regime**:

```text
admitted candidate
        ↓
equality with the canonical perimeter
```

Conversely, the canonical perimeter has an admission witness.

Thus:

```text
admission in the regime
↔
equality with the canonical perimeter
```

### 4.8 Constitutive boundary and relative maximality

The free continuation beyond the perimeter really exists.

But because it is strictly different from the canonical perimeter, exact regime classification implies that it cannot be admitted in the same regime.

More generally, no strict extension of the perimeter can remain in that regime.

```text
canonical perimeter
        ↓
strict extension possible
        ↓
exit from the regime
```

The correct notion is therefore that of a **constitutive boundary of the regime** or **relative maximality of the perimeter**.

The construction continues.

The regime does not continue with it.

## 5. Derived structural length

Length enters only after the constitutive structure of the perimeter and its relative maximality have been established.

The constitutive function of the perimeter is therefore already fully established before any numerical measurement.

Length is not a principle of constitution.

It measures a construction already obtained.

```text
constitution of the perimeter
        ↓
classification / maximality
        ↓
length measurement
```

A constitutive-prefix relation implies an order relation on lengths:

```text
A is a prefix of B
        ↓
length(A) ≤ length(B)
```

A strict extension implies:

```text
A is a strict prefix of B
        ↓
length(A) < length(B)
```

Admissible partial constructions therefore cannot be longer than the perimeter.

And every construction belonging to the same circular regime has exactly the length of the canonical perimeter.

But this logical order must remain explicit:

> **Equality of lengths is never used to identify a history with the canonical perimeter; it is obtained only after that identification has already been established structurally.**

In other words:

```text
not:
same length
→ same perimeter

but:
same constitutive history as the perimeter
→ same length
```

Length is therefore **derived from constitutive structure**, not used to produce it.

This reverses the usual explanatory order:

```text
not:
length
→ perimeter

but:
constituted perimeter
→ relative maximality
→ derived length
```

---

In what follows, the terms **identity**, **constitutive determination**, and **alignment** provide a theoretical reading of explicit formal structures and equations. The Lean development directly verifies the occurrences, indexings, exact transports, extensions, compositions, and naturality laws that constrain this reading. These terms are not introduced as additional primitive predicates independent of those constructions.

## 6. Identity and constitutive indexing

Once occurrences have been constituted, determined, and distinguished, the question of their identity through transformations can be posed.

> What does it mean to say that this is still the same determination elsewhere?

Identity is not defined by equality of readouts, values, representations, or statuses.

It is understood as the **demonstrated persistence of an already constituted identity when it remains tracked by its constitutive indexing or by its canonical prolongation under extension**.

### 6.1 Genesis of indexing

Indexing comes after constitution.

It is not computed from a value or a readout.

Nor does it replace the constitutive role.

```text
construction
        ↓
constituted occurrences
        ↓
indexing structure
        ↓
tracking of identities
```

The constitutive role answers:

> What determines this occurrence?

Indexing answers:

> How can this occurrence be followed once it has been constituted?

One must therefore distinguish:

```text
constitutive determination
≠
identity indexing
```

When a construction is extended, old identities are preserved and a new identity may appear.

```text
identities at stage n
        ↓
extension
        ↓
old identities preserved
+
new identity
```

Identities introduced at distinct stages remain distinct when transported into a common later depth.

### 6.2 Three carrier levels

The theory distinguishes three levels that must not be conflated.

The first is the **canonical identity-indexing carrier**.

In the generic theory, its initial carrier is abstract. At every extension, this structure preserves the identities already indexed and adds one new identity.

The second is the **constitutive carrier of occurrences** of a particular construction.

The third is the **concrete realization carrier**.

```text
canonical indexing carrier
        ↕
constitutive occurrence carrier
        ↕
concrete realization carrier
```

The canonical carrier provides the common reference through which identities can be followed.

The constitutive carrier contains the occurrences actually constituted in the construction under consideration.

The concrete carrier contains their particular realization.

In the circular instance, the connection becomes explicit: **the initial carrier of constituted occurrences is the carrier of the already reconstructed perimeter**, and successive extensions then add the new occurrences produced by the construction.

Thus:

```text
perimeter occurrences
        ↓
initial carrier of the circular instance
        ↓
extension
        ↓
old occurrences
+
new occurrence
```

The canonical indexing carrier is then connected exactly to these actually constituted occurrences of the generated histories.

These levels are exactly related, but they are not identified.

The same identity can therefore receive several realizations without being defined by any one of them.

### 6.3 The residual as the identity being tracked

The circular instance provides a particularly important case.

The fresh identity introduced by the canonical constitutive transition is exactly the residual occurrence determined earlier.

```text
unique residual occurrence
=
fresh identity of the transition
```

This identification therefore does not rest on a resemblance between two independently constructed objects.

In this instance, the two descriptions connect to the same constitutive object.

Thus, when the theory moves to persistence, it does not begin with an abstract identifier of indeterminate origin.

It follows an occurrence whose constitution, role, and uniqueness have already been established.

## 7. Persistence

Persistence concerns two kinds of change: change of realization and extension of the construction.

Before studying them, two uses of the term **exactness** must be distinguished in the theory.

In circular reconstruction, **local constitutive exactness** expresses agreement between a structural requirement and the occurrence that realizes it.

In the theory of persistence, an **exact transport** is a reversible correspondence between two carriers.

```text
local constitutive exactness
≠
exact transport
```

These notions cooperate in the general architecture, but they are not the same formal structure.

### 7.1 Exact transport

An **exact transport** is a bidirectional correspondence between two carriers such that every element can be recovered exactly after a round trip.

```text
Source  ──forward──▶  Target
Source  ◀─backward──  Target

backward(forward(x)) = x
forward(backward(y)) = y
```

This exactness guarantees that transported elements are neither lost nor merged.

But it is not sufficient, by itself, to establish persistence of a constituted identity.

A correspondence can be perfectly exact while permuting two identities.

One must therefore distinguish:

```text
arbitrary exact transport
≠
induced identity transport
```

### 7.2 Change of realization

At a given constitutive depth, two realizations are coordinated through the same canonical index.

```text
occurrence in A
        ↓
shared constitutive index
        ↓
occurrence in B
```

Identity transport is therefore not obtained by directly comparing the two concrete objects.

It returns from the source occurrence to its constitutive index and then realizes **that same index** in the target carrier.

```text
A(i)
        ↓
i
        ↓
B(i)
```

The correspondence is thus determined by the shared constitutive identity.

### 7.3 Extension of the construction

When depth changes, the earlier identity receives a canonical image in the later carrier.

```text
identity at depth n
        ↓
canonical extension
        ↓
same identity at a later depth
```

This extension is injective.

Previously established distinctions are therefore preserved.

An earlier identity does not merge with a fresh identity, and two identities introduced at different depths do not become equal in a common later carrier.

### 7.4 Two axes of persistence

There are two directions:

```text
horizontal:
change of realization
→ same index

vertical:
extension
→ canonical image of the identity
```

These two directions must be compatible.

### 7.5 Preservation of identity and preservation of properties

Persistence of an identity does not automatically transport all properties associated with that identity.

```text
identity preserved
≠
all properties preserved
```

Constitutive transport follows the identity.

Preservation of an order, role, provenance, readout, value, or status requires an additional proof.

```text
identity
        ↓
constitutive transport
        ↓
persistence

additional property
        ↓
specific preservation proof
        ↓
preservation of that property
```

Thus:

```text
same determination
≠ same representation
≠ same value
≠ same status
```

An occurrence can change realization or status without losing the constitutive identity being tracked.

## 8. Alignment

Alignment is not the starting point of the theory.

It appears only after constitution, indexing, and persistence.

For identity transports to constitute an alignment, they must be coherent.

### 8.1 Composition of changes of realization

A composed passage must agree with the direct passage:

```text
A → B → C
=
A → C
```

Introducing an intermediate realization must not change the identity obtained at the destination.

### 8.2 Composition of extensions

Two successive extensions must agree with their composition:

```text
depth n
→ depth m
→ depth p

=

depth n
→ depth p
```

Introducing an intermediate depth must not change the transported identity.

### 8.3 Naturality between extension and realization

The two axes must commute:

```text
extension
then change of realization

=

change of realization
then extension
```

In other words:

```text
realization A at depth s
        ───── extension ─────→
realization A at depth t
        │                         │
        │ transport               │ transport
        ↓                         ↓
realization B at depth s
        ───── extension ─────→
realization B at depth t
```

This condition is essential.

An arbitrary exact bijection can preserve round trips while permuting fresh identities and making this square fail.

Coherence is therefore not an automatic consequence of exactness.

It expresses compatibility of transports with constitutive indexing.

### 8.4 Definition of alignment

Within this framework:

> **alignment is the coherence of identity transports induced by a shared constitutive indexing, through which an already constituted identity is tracked across extensions and distinct realizations.**

The complete chain is therefore:

```text
constitution
        ↓
relational constitutive role
        ↓
dependencies
        ↓
separation and reconstruction
        ↓
circular instance
        ↓
perimeter reconstruction
        ↓
delimitation of the internal
        ↓
continuation
        ↓
residual and uniqueness
        ↓
exact regime classification
        ↓
constitutive boundary
        ↓
derived length
        ↓
identity
        ↓
indexing
        ↓
persistence
        ↓
coherence
        ↓
alignment
```

Alignment therefore does not constitute identity.

**It establishes the coherence of the transports through which an already constituted identity is tracked across realizations and extensions.**
